\baselineskip 0.30in \vskip 0.05in \noi {\bf Abstract:}  Data defined on the discrete set $n=\{0,1,..., m\}$ widely exists in biomedicine, finance, engineering, social sciences and other fields. As the basic model for fitting such data, binomial distribution has the advantages of simple mathematical form and clear statistical significance, but its inherent assumptions lead to limited fitting effect for the over-dispersion phenomenon that is common in the real data. The Bayesians reconstructs the success probability $p$ of binomial distribution into a random variable, and uses beta distribution as a prior to derive beta--binomial distribution. The model not only overcomes the fitting defect of binomial distribution to over-dispersion data, significantly improves the robustness, but also has good parameter interpretation, which has become a popular tool in various fields. However, the subjective choice of its prior distribution may lead to the deviation of prior information representation, which is difficult to adapt to the diversity characteristics of complex real data. This paper proposes an accurate prior framework for constructing binomial distribution success probability based on Bernstein polynomial, which can flexibly adapt to all kinds of prior information characteristics, and establishes the corresponding theoretical system of statistical inference and Bayesian inference. Simulation experiments and real data applications verify the superiority and practicability of the proposed distribution and method.

\vkL \noi {\bf Keywords:}   Bayesian inference, Bernstein polynomial, Binomial distribution, MM algorithm, Quantile regression model.
